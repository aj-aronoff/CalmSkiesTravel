2. Website name: Calm Skies Travel, with the tagline "Safe travels, calm skies". I own calmskiestravel.com, .info and .cloud.
3. We are going to build a website for autistic children and parents, with the approach, that I have learned for successful travel.
4. The website will have "One guide, two voices": Every topic has a  checklists. A) One for the parents B) It has a Child section in simple, first-person words that can be tailored to each child. The child always comes first: the guide adapts to them, not the other way around.
4. Social stories that child and parent can customize (the heart of the project): The Story Builder turns in the child's name, where they're going, who they'll see and what step-by-step social story to read together, print or laminate. This is the most important feature.
5. Hello and Thank you page. A page with somem easy multiple choices, saying hello to the people being visted or the hotel being stayed at as well as a thank you after the trip, and an I enjoyed going to destination, with child's name, destination and picture.
6. Every stage of the trip: Prep before the flight, the airport, The plane ride, the destination, Going home. Each stage has a plan for the parent and one for the child.
7. Pack the calm, ship the rest: The "go bag" (chewelry, headphones, preloaded music, snacks and the social story). Consider ship food and supplies ahead, for example by Amazon to the hotel or a relative's home. Then familiar things are already in when you get back.
8. When it gets hard (the meltdown plan):
   - Before: spot the triggers early.
   - During: keep the child safe, cut down noise and light, use fewer words and find a quiet spot.
   - After: a comfort item, water (in a reusable bottle). No blame. Note the trigger for next time.
9. The Sunflower lanyard: The Hidden Disabilities Sunflower tells airport and airline staff quietly that a traveler
   may need extra time, patience or help, win.
10. Help that's easy to miss:
   - TSA Cares: call about 72 hours before tsecurity.
   - The airline's disability code (DPNA): ask the airline to add it to the booking.
   - Pre-boarding and seat choice.
   - Airport "Wings for Autism" rehearsals.
   - Airport sensory rooms.
   Most of this has to be set up BEFORE the flight
11. A predictable schedule: Travel breaks routines, so the guide keeps the parts of the routine you can and makes the rest visible. That means schedule extra time built in, time-zone planning, and a plan ready if the flight is delayed.
12. Communication strategies (suggestion from an autism support group): Open ended questions. May need to simplify. Please tell me more. Silence and mirroring. Can I share that back with you so that person feels heard and understood. What would you tell someone flying. What support might you need.
